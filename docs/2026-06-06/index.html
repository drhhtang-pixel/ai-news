Agentic AI is the defining architectural shift of 2025. The industry has moved decisively from single-purpose chatbots to multi-agent systems capable of autonomous, multi-step reasoning and action — booking purchases, managing workflows, and collaborating across enterprise tools. IBM, OpenAI, and dozens of startups are all racing to define what "agentic" means in practice. However, the gap between hype and reality remains significant: customer service and code generation are clear wins, while fully autonomous enterprise agents still face reliability and trust hurdles.

The regulatory environment is fracturing — and getting urgent. A major fault line has opened between the federal government and U.S. states on AI governance. The Trump administration's deregulatory executive order is on a collision course with California's and Colorado's comprehensive AI laws, setting up potential constitutional battles. At the same time, the EU AI Act is now binding, forcing global companies to comply with transparency and safety mandates. Perhaps most alarmingly, the explosion of AI-generated harmful content — with CSAM reports exceeding one million — is accelerating calls for stronger safety guardrails, regardless of political ideology. The question of *who* governs AI, and *how*, is now as urgent as the technology itself.
